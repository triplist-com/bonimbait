Notes (all E unless sourced):
- Time includes permits? No: structure start to keys, excluding permit (permit takes months; occupancy form
  up to ~6 months, per nadlancenter).
- Israeli general cost anchors: building cost ~5,000-6,000 ILS per m2 at average standard, shell counted on pour
  area [nadlancenter]; shell work 1,800-2,800 per built m2 [midrag]; light construction 2,500-5,000 per m2
  (additions) [midrag]. Nadlancenter also says conventional and steel frames have "relatively similar costs".
- Standards: ת״י 413 (seismic design), ת״י 1045 (thermal insulation of buildings), ת״י 466 (concrete),
  ת״י 4422 (ממ״ד doors/windows/shutters). Light steel and timber frames need a structural engineer's design
  to ת״י 413; there is no single prescriptive "light construction" code for private houses, so each project
  relies on engineer plus the committee. ICF: no dedicated Israeli code (designed as RC to ת״י 466). Precast:
  ת״י 466 plus manufacturer approvals; factories exist (e.g. precast firms serving housing) (E).
- ממ״ד: the permit must include a protected space in new houses regardless of method [midrag]; Home Front
  Command approves the plan. In practice the ממ״ד is reinforced concrete (cast-in-place) even in light
  houses; approved precast modules exist but are rare (E: verify with פיקוד העורף before promising).
- Mortgage/appraisal: banks pay in stages against surveyor reports [nadlancenter]. Light frames can face
  lower appraisal and harder insurance quotes (E); concrete and precast are neutral.
- Maintenance: LSF/timber need care for waterproofing details, roof leaks, and wall penetrations; timber needs
  termite and moisture control. ICF/precast/concrete: little beyond waterproofing and joints.
- Known suppliers (E, verify): GSB Systems (ICF forms, gsbsystems.co.il), local LSF fabricators, imported
  timber-panel firms, precast plants (concrete producers) and crane rental firms.
